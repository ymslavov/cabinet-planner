# Construction — how a cabinet is built

Generated by `src/engine/parts.ts` → `cabinetParts(cabinet, dims, settings)`. The same
part list draws the 3D model and feeds the cut plan, so this file, the 3D view and the
cut list must always agree.

## The OSB rules (from the planning sheet — they still apply)

- OSB has almost no **edge** screw-holding: never screw into a sheet edge. Panels are screwed
  through their faces into cleat faces.
- Every OSB cut is a straight rip or crosscut — no router, no dados, no notches. This is why
  shelves stop short of the corner cleats instead of being notched around them.
- A full glued-and-screwed back panel is what stops the box racking under a rolling load.
  Turning `hasBack` off is allowed but the checks warn.
- Tops are laminated from `topLayers` (default 2) layers glued face to face — one layer sags
  under the saw and won't hold T-track or bolts.
- Casters bolt through the bottom panel and a cleat with penny washers and nyloc nuts,
  never wood screws.
- Seal every cut edge and both faces of the tops (thinned PVA, shellac or oil paint): bare OSB
  swells and delaminates in a workshop.
- Nominal 15 mm OSB measures 14.5–15.5 — enter the gauged thickness in Settings.

## Coordinate frame

Cabinet space: origin at the footprint centre on the floor, X = width, Y = up, Z = depth,
**+Z = the open front** (the operator side). Casters occupy `0..casterHeight`.

## Panels (all build methods)

| Part | Size | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom | W × L | on the casters, full footprint |
| Back | W × (H − t) | stands on the bottom, full width, behind the sides |
| Side ×2 | (L − t) × (H − t) | stand on the bottom, between back and front edge |
| Top layer ×topLayers | W × L | on top of sides/back/cleats |
| Shelf ×n | (W − 2t) × depth | rests on a side rail pair; depth runs from the rear cleats to the front (frame: to the front posts) |

W, L = cabinet outside width/length; H = carcass height; t = measured sheet thickness.

## Framing (cleat cross-section ca × cb, ca = thin side facing into the box)

| Method | Section | Material in cut plan |
|---|---|---|
| `osb-cleat` | (laminations × t) × osbCleat.width = 45 × 60 | `laminations` OSB strips of width × length per cleat, nested on sheets |
| `timber-cleat` | timber a × b = 30 × 40 | timber lengths |
| `timber-frame` | 30 × 40 | timber lengths |

Members, all methods (rear ones only when there is a back, except timber-frame which always has them):

- Rear corner vertical ×2 — cb along X against the side, ca along Z against the back, full inside height.
- Back rail bottom/top — between the rear verticals.
- Side rail bottom/top ×2 — from the rear verticals to the front edge.
- Shelf rail ×2 per shelf — directly under the shelf.

timber-frame adds: front post ×2 (open-face corners) and front rail bottom/top between them.
Side rails and shelves then stop behind the front posts.

## Shelves

`cabinet.shelves` holds each shelf's **top-face height above the inside floor**. A shelf is
generated only inside `shelfRange` = `[t + 2·cb, (H − t) − cb]` — its rail must clear the
bottom rail and it must clear the top rail. Out-of-range shelves are dropped (checks warn).

## Setting the height: shims under the tool (`settings.shimAllowance`)

Why: the decks must meet the outfeed cabinets exactly, and a real build won't land on 900 to
the millimetre. The cabinets always stand on their casters (Y, 2026-09-26), so the fine
adjustment happens between the cabinet top and the tool.

- Every cabinet whose tool is on the shared plane is built **`shimAllowance` (10 mm) low**:
  `surface = target − deck − allowance`. The tool sits on four **slotted (horseshoe) shim
  stacks**, one at each corner of its base, nominally `allowance` thick — so the deck still
  lands at 900.
- To trim: loosen the tool's bolts, slide shims out or in, re-tighten. Range: no shims →
  900 − 10, double stack → 900 + 10 (`dims.adjust`).
- Shims: plastic construction packers or hardboard, 1 / 2 / 3 / 5 mm; the slot lets them go in
  around the bolts without unbolting the tool.
- Not shimmed: exempt tools (the drill press — its table adjusts) and plain cabinets without a
  tool (their top *is* the work surface, at the target height).

**History:** v3 of the app had DIY levelling feet (threaded rods in OSB blocks lifting the
cabinet off its casters). Y rejected that design — the cabinets stay on casters — and it was
removed in schema v4.

## Degenerate cabinets

No parts when the carcass is ≤ 0, too short for top and bottom rails (`H − t < 2·cb + 1`),
too narrow for the back rails (`W − 2t − 2·cb ≤ 0`), or with no depth left between the rear
framing and the front (`zFront − zRear ≤ 0` — only timber-frame has framing at the front).
The checks report why.
